1. Task Overview — Cube Lift vs Pick-and-Place

Pick-and-Place (PnP) is fundamentally harder than Cube Lift for residual RL. This section analyzes why Lift achieved 100% SR from 18% base but PnP residual cannot exceed the 72% base policy.

DimensionCube LiftPick-and-PlaceImpact
Task Phases1 phase: reach → grasp → lift4+ phases: reach → grasp → lift → transport → place → settleMuch harder
Success CriteriaCube lifted ≥ 4cmCube XY ≤ 9.5cm of bowl AND Z ≤ 5.5cm AND velocity ≤ 0.05 m/s3 simultaneous conditions
Base Policy SR18–48% (hard table)72% (hard positions)Less room to improve
Reward before successUp to ~0.8 (continuous height shaping)Up to 0.4 (approach+grasp+lift+transport)2× less intermediate signal
Success reward jumpGradual (height → success)Binary +0.6 (place or nothing)Sparse-like
Typical episode length50–150 steps200–500 stepsLonger credit assignment
Eval positions10 positions15 positions (wider spread + bowl)Higher variance
Episode horizon500 steps500 stepsSame
Action space7D (pos3 + euler3 + grip1)7D (pos3 + euler3 + grip1)Same
Core Problem: PnP's reward is sparse-in-disguise. The dense_clipped reward gives max 0.4 for pre-success stages, then a binary +0.6 for place success. The actor gets almost no gradient for improving transport/place — the key phases where base policy fails. In contrast, Lift's height reward provides continuous gradient at every step.
The "already good" base problem: Base PnP policy is at 72% SR. The residual adds ±0.02m perturbation to every action. For the 72% of trajectories that already succeed, residual can only hurt (add noise to good trajectories). For Cube Lift at 18% base SR, almost any learned correction had upside.

5. Key Insights & Lessons

✅ What works:
  • L2 = 3-5 keeps residual alive but controlled
  • action_scale = 0.1 better than 0.3 (less noise on good trajectories)
  • Dense_clipped reward better than sparse for actor gradient
  • Residual makes successful trajectories 10% faster
  • Can solve some positions that base fails on (env 2, 14)
❌ What doesn't work:
  • L2 ≥ 10 kills residual completely (Res → 0)
  • Sparse reward gives 10× weaker actor gradient than dense
  • High γ (0.99, 0.995) doesn't compensate for sparse signal
  • Offline data with raw rewards (up to 107) causes Q explosion
  • Residual can degrade already-successful trajectories

Why E3 works best

L2=5.0 is the "conservative sweet spot": Residual stays small (Res_max ≈ 0.01-0.03) — enough to correct marginal failures but not enough to disrupt good trajectories. Lower L2 (1.0, 3.0) produces larger residuals that sometimes help more but also break previously-successful envs. E3 is the only config where no successful base env regressed in single-seed eval.

Multi-seed context

In 5-seed evaluation, E3 averages 73.3% ± 9.4% vs base 72.0% ± 2.7%. The single-seed result (80% vs 67%) represents a favorable seed; on other seeds E3 can regress on some envs. The higher variance (±9.4% vs ±2.7%) indicates the residual introduces stochasticity — it helps some envs and occasionally hurts others depending on evaluation noise.

7. Root Cause Analysis — Why PnP Residual Improvement is Limited

Three fundamental barriers:

  1. Reward is sparse-in-disguise: Pre-success stages give max 0.4 total reward. The 0.6 place-success bonus is binary — no gradient for "almost placed." The actor learns to grasp faster (where reward is dense) but cannot learn to place better (where reward is binary).
  2. High base SR limits upside: At 72% base SR, residual must output near-zero on 72% of trajectories (to not hurt them) while making large corrections on the remaining 28%. This is a harder optimization than uniformly improving all trajectories (as in Lift at 18%).
  3. Credit assignment gap: From grasp to place-success is ~100-200 steps. With n_step=3 and γ=0.9, the effective discount is 0.9^150 ≈ 10⁻⁷. The place reward signal cannot propagate back to early-episode actions that determine transport quality.

8. Round 4: dense_v3 Reward + 66ep Recollected Offline Data (current)

Major fix: Rounds 1-3 used offline data collected with reward_type="dense" (scale 0~107), while training used dense_clipped (0~1). In Round 4, we redesigned rewards (dense_v3) and recollected offline data with correct rewards, solving the reward mismatch that undermined all prior offline experiments.

8.1 Reward Redesign: dense_v3

New dense_v3 reward explicitly covers all PnP phases with continuous shaping, eliminating the "sparse-in-disguise" problem identified in Section 7.

ComponentWeightDescription
Approach0.15Distance TCP → cube (continuous)
Alignment0.10Gripper-cube orientation match (continuous)
Grasp0.10Contact detection
Lift0.10Cube height above table
Transport0.30Distance cube → bowl (continuous shaping)
Success0.25Place success criteria met
Total1.00All phases covered, no binary jumps
Key improvements over dense_clipped: (1) Transport phase gets 0.30 weight with continuous distance shaping — no more binary 0.6 jump. (2) Alignment reward encourages proper grasp angle. (3) All components sum to exactly 1.0 — no need for clamping.

8.5 Key Observations

🏆 Job 53895 (L2=1, γ=0.95, of=0.75) achieves 86.7% SR — the highest SR ever observed in PnP experiments. From 53% base SR, this represents a +33.4%p improvement. The combination of larger residual freedom (L2=1) and long-horizon discount (γ=0.95) enables the actor to learn meaningful transport corrections. With the corrected dense_v3 reward providing continuous transport shaping, the credit assignment problem from Round 3 is significantly reduced.
dense_v3 reward fixes the core problem: All 5 normal-difficulty jobs show improvement over their respective base SR. Compare with Rounds 1-3 where most configs degraded from base. The continuous transport reward (0.30 weight) provides the gradient signal that was missing in dense_clipped's binary 0.6 success bonus.
SR oscillates ±13%p between evals: With 15 eval envs, each env flip = ±6.7%p. Job 53895 hit 86.7% once at 14k but oscillates 60-80% at other steps. This matches Round 3 variance observations. Multi-seed evaluation would be needed to confirm the improvement is statistically significant.
Hard difficulty remains challenging: Best hard job (53898) reached 46.7% peak (from 20% base), but the improvement is unstable (latest: 33%). With action_scale=0.1, the residual cannot make large enough corrections for extreme positions. Potential fixes: increase action_scale, curriculum from normal→hard, or collect more hard-specific offline data.
