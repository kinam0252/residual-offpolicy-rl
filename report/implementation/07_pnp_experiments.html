1. Task Overview — Cube Lift vs Pick-and-Place

Pick-and-Place (PnP) is fundamentally harder than Cube Lift for residual RL. This section analyzes why Lift achieved 100% SR from 18% base but PnP residual cannot exceed the 72% base policy.

DimensionCube LiftPick-and-PlaceImpact
Task Phases1 phase: reach → grasp → lift4+ phases: reach → grasp → lift → transport → place → settleMuch harder
Success CriteriaCube lifted ≥ 4cmCube XY ≤ 9.5cm of bowl AND Z ≤ 5.5cm AND velocity ≤ 0.05 m/s3 simultaneous conditions
Base Policy SR18–48% (hard table)72% (hard positions)Less room to improve
Reward before successUp to ~0.8 (continuous height shaping)Up to 0.4 (approach+grasp+lift+transport)2× less intermediate signal
Success reward jumpGradual (height → success)Binary +0.6 (place or nothing)Sparse-like
Typical episode length50–150 steps200–500 stepsLonger credit assignment
Eval positions10 positions15 positions (wider spread + bowl)Higher variance
Episode horizon500 steps500 stepsSame
Action space7D (pos3 + euler3 + grip1)7D (pos3 + euler3 + grip1)Same
Core Problem: PnP's reward is sparse-in-disguise. The dense_clipped reward gives max 0.4 for pre-success stages, then a binary +0.6 for place success. The actor gets almost no gradient for improving transport/place — the key phases where base policy fails. In contrast, Lift's height reward provides continuous gradient at every step.
The "already good" base problem: Base PnP policy is at 72% SR. The residual adds ±0.02m perturbation to every action. For the 72% of trajectories that already succeed, residual can only hurt (add noise to good trajectories). For Cube Lift at 18% base SR, almost any learned correction had upside.

5. Key Insights & Lessons

✅ What works:
  • L2 = 3-5 keeps residual alive but controlled
  • action_scale = 0.1 better than 0.3 (less noise on good trajectories)
  • Dense_clipped reward better than sparse for actor gradient
  • Residual makes successful trajectories 10% faster
  • Can solve some positions that base fails on (env 2, 14)
❌ What doesn't work:
  • L2 ≥ 10 kills residual completely (Res → 0)
  • Sparse reward gives 10× weaker actor gradient than dense
  • High γ (0.99, 0.995) doesn't compensate for sparse signal
  • Offline data with raw rewards (up to 107) causes Q explosion
  • Residual can degrade already-successful trajectories

6. Root Cause Analysis — Why PnP Residual Doesn't Work

Three fundamental barriers:

  1. Reward is sparse-in-disguise: Pre-success stages give max 0.4 total reward. The 0.6 place-success bonus is binary — no gradient for "almost placed." The actor learns to grasp faster (where reward is dense) but cannot learn to place better (where reward is binary).
  2. High base SR limits upside: At 72% base SR, residual must output near-zero on 72% of trajectories (to not hurt them) while making large corrections on the remaining 28%. This is a harder optimization than uniformly improving all trajectories (as in Lift at 18%).
  3. Credit assignment gap: From grasp to place-success is ~100-200 steps. With n_step=3 and γ=0.9, the effective discount is 0.9^150 ≈ 10⁻⁷. The place reward signal cannot propagate back to early-episode actions that determine transport quality.
