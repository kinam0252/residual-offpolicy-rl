Conclusions:
  1. Residual improves task efficiency — successful episodes complete 5–21% faster with much lower variance, indicating more decisive and consistent execution.
  2. Residual helps on hard positions — positions where base struggles (0–33% SR) see the biggest gains (up to +33%p), converting failures into successes.
  3. Residual can regress on easy positions — positions where base already succeeds reliably sometimes degrade. This suggests the residual over-corrects when correction isn't needed.
  4. Residual increases at task completion — temporal analysis shows the residual's main contribution is during the final phase (grasp/place/stack), providing precision corrections that the base policy lacks.
  5. Action smoothness is preserved or improved — the residual does not introduce jitter; PnP grip jerk is 13.5% lower, and rotation smoothness improves by 3–8%.
